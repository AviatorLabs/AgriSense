## 2. Team-wide responsibilities

Every member is expected to:
- Understand the product goals and the feature they are working on.
- Participate in planning, implementation, testing, and review.
- Communicate progress and blockers early.
- Use GitHub issues, branches, and pull requests.
- Review teammates' work and provide constructive feedback.
- Document important decisions and setup discoveries.
- Continue learning React, Next.js, JavaScript, MongoDB, and the team's tools.

## 3. Team Lead and Coordinator

The Team Lead is responsible for:
- Keeping the project aligned with the assignment, agreed scope, and timeline.
- Facilitating planning and weekly engineering reviews.
- Helping break large goals into manageable issues.
- Monitoring dependencies, risks, and blockers.
- Coordinating communication and ensuring decisions are recorded.
- Supporting code review and integration without becoming the sole decision-maker.
- Delegating meaningful ownership and helping members grow.

The Team Lead should not become the only person who understands the architecture or is allowed to merge work. Knowledge and responsibility should be shared.

## 4. Feature-based ownership

Permanent frontend/backend specialization is not required. Assign work by feature or vertical slice so members gain experience across the stack.

For example, a farm-management feature may involve:
- UI and interaction design.
- React/Next.js implementation.
- Server-side validation and business logic.
- Mongoose model and database operations.
- Tests and documentation.

One member may coordinate a feature while others contribute or pair on unfamiliar parts.

## 5. Supporting responsibilities

As the project develops, the team may rotate or assign supporting responsibilities:
- UI/UX consistency and accessibility.
- Database schema and data integrity.
- GitHub issue/PR hygiene.
- Testing and quality checks.
- Documentation and demo preparation.
- Deployment and environment configuration.

These are shared team responsibilities, not permanent job titles.

## 6. Assignment approach

Individual feature owners will be assigned after the team has reviewed:
- Current availability and workload.
- Personal interests and learning goals.
- Existing strengths and areas each member wants to improve.
- Feature dependencies and required collaboration.

Do not assign all unfamiliar technical work to one person. Use pairing, small tasks, and review to spread knowledge.

## 7. Communication and meetings

- Telegram is the primary channel for daily communication.
- Members should provide concise progress updates: completed, next, and blocked.
- GitHub is the authoritative place for tasks, code, PRs, and technical decisions.
- The weekly AviatorLabs engineering review is scheduled for Friday at 13:00 Africa/Addis_Ababa.
- Meeting outcomes and action items should be recorded in GitHub.

## 8. Collaboration expectations

- Ask for help early; being blocked is normal, hiding a blocker is not.
- Explain technical choices so teammates can learn.
- Be respectful in reviews and disagreements.
- Prefer evidence, prototypes, and small experiments when choosing between approaches.
- Share knowledge and avoid creating single-person dependencies.
- Raise scope or schedule concerns as soon as they appear.
